What I test

A fluent answer is not necessarily a sound one. I make the judgment criteria explicit.

Factual support

Do the sources and known facts actually support the answer's claims?

Instruction adherence

Did the response follow the stated task, constraints, format, and audience?

Reasoning quality

Does the conclusion follow from the evidence, or has the model smuggled in an assumption?

Calibration

Does confidence match the available evidence, uncertainty, and consequences of being wrong?

Linguistic quality

Is the writing clear and natural without synthetic phrasing, empty polish, or evasive abstraction?

Underlying usefulness

Does the answer solve the user's real problem rather than merely responding to the surface request?

Worked evaluation sample

Revenue forecast with impressive arithmetic and unsupported certainty

Representative task A founder asks an AI system to estimate first-year revenue for a new subscription product using a market-size figure, an assumed conversion rate, and a monthly price.

Unsupported conversion assumption

The calculation is internally consistent, but the response treats an invented conversion rate as established demand.

False precision

A single revenue number conceals uncertainty in acquisition, churn, pricing tolerance, and sales capacity.

Missing decision context

The user needs a validation plan and scenario range, not reassurance disguised as forecasting.

Better response

Separate known inputs from assumptions, show conservative/base/upside scenarios, identify the variables that drive the result, and recommend the cheapest test that could replace an assumption with evidence.

Verdict: numerically tidy, evidentially weak. The correct intervention is calibration—not prettier prose.

How I work

  1. Clarify the task.Identify the intended outcome, audience, constraints, and consequences of error.
  2. Separate claim types.Distinguish established fact, inference, interpretation, recommendation, and speculation.
  3. Stress the answer.Test edge cases, missing context, contradictory evidence, and places where fluency masks a gap.
  4. Explain the judgment.Record not only what failed, but why it failed and what a stronger response must do.
  5. Protect usefulness.Preserve nuance while making the result clearer, more accurate, and more actionable.
